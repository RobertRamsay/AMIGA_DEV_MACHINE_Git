=== UNIT 9: Blitter, sprites, sound and input

=== LESSON 81: The blitter: copying a block
@summary Agnus's blitter: channels A to D, a minterm, and BLTSIZE to start it.
@text
The blitter is a DMA engine in Agnus that moves and combines rectangles of memory while the CPU does something else. It reads up to three sources, A, B and C, combines them with a logic function (the minterm) and writes the result to D.

To copy a block, use A as the source and D as the destination, and the minterm $F0, which means "D = A":

	BLTCON0	$09F0	USEA ($0800) + USED ($0100) + minterm $F0
	BLTCON1	$0000
	BLTAFWM / BLTALWM	$FFFF	first / last word masks: keep everything
	BLTAPT, BLTDPT		source and destination addresses
	BLTAMOD, BLTDMOD	bytes to skip after each row
	BLTSIZE	height << 6 | width in words: writing it starts the blit

The modulo lets you copy a rectangle out of a wider screen: a block 2 words wide on a 40-byte-wide screen skips 40 - 4 = 36 bytes after each row.

Before touching the blitter's registers, wait until it has finished its last job: test BBUSY (bit 14 of DMACONR, bit 6 of its high byte). On real hardware, read DMACONR once before the loop (an Agnus quirk):

	tst.b	DMACONR(a6)
waitblit:
	btst	#6,DMACONR(a6)
	bne	waitblit
@task
Copy a block 2 words wide and 4 rows high from the screen at A0 to the screen at A1. Both screens are 40 bytes wide. Wait for the blitter first.
@start
	lea	CUSTOM,a6
	; wait for the blitter, set it up, start it with BLTSIZE

@solution
	lea	CUSTOM,a6
	tst.b	DMACONR(a6)
waitblit:
	btst	#6,DMACONR(a6)
	bne	waitblit
	move.w	#$09F0,BLTCON0(a6)
	move.w	#0,BLTCON1(a6)
	move.w	#$FFFF,BLTAFWM(a6)
	move.w	#$FFFF,BLTALWM(a6)
	move.l	a0,BLTAPT(a6)
	move.l	a1,BLTDPT(a6)
	move.w	#36,BLTAMOD(a6)
	move.w	#36,BLTDMOD(a6)
	move.w	#(4<<6)+2,BLTSIZE(a6)
@hint
BLTCON0 = $09F0, both masks $FFFF, the two pointers as longs, both modulos 36, BLTSIZE = (4 << 6) + 2 - written last.
@test A 32 x 4 block
setup: a0=20000 a1=30000 m20000.l=11112222 m20028.l=33334444 m20050.l=55556666 m20078.l=77778888 m30004.l=FFFFFFFF m300A0.l=FFFFFFFF
expect: m30000.l=11112222 m30028.l=33334444 m30050.l=55556666 m30078.l=77778888 m30004.l=FFFFFFFF m300A0.l=FFFFFFFF uses=btst
@quiz
? Which register starts the blit?
- BLTCON0
+ BLTSIZE
- BLTDPT
? What does the minterm $F0 mean?
+ D = A
- D = B
- D = 0
? A blit is 3 words wide on a 40-byte-wide screen. What is the modulo?
- 3
- 6
+ 34

=== LESSON 82: Blitter shifts and masks
@summary Moving graphics to any pixel position: the A shift and the last word mask.
@text
Graphics rarely sit on a 16-pixel boundary. The blitter shifts channel A right by 0 to 15 pixels as it copies: the shift goes in the top four bits of BLTCON0 (ASH). Bits pushed out of one word move into the next, so a shifted image needs one extra word of width.

That extra word reads one word too many from the source, so the last word mask clears it: BLTALWM = $0000. Only the bits shifted in from the previous word land in the extra word. To make the source pointer come back to the next row properly, its modulo is -2: it read one word too far.

	BLTCON0	$49F0	ASH = 4, A and D, D = A
	BLTALWM	$0000
	BLTAMOD	-2
	BLTSIZE	rows << 6 | 2

BOBs (blitter objects) are drawn exactly like this, plus a mask and the background (lesson 85).
@task
A0 points at an image 1 word wide and 3 rows high (2 bytes per row). Copy it 4 pixels to the right into the screen at A1 (40 bytes wide): the destination is 2 words wide. Wait for the blitter first.
@start
	lea	CUSTOM,a6
	; shift the 16 x 3 image at A0 right by 4 into the screen at A1

@solution
	lea	CUSTOM,a6
	tst.b	DMACONR(a6)
waitblit:
	btst	#6,DMACONR(a6)
	bne	waitblit
	move.w	#$49F0,BLTCON0(a6)
	move.w	#0,BLTCON1(a6)
	move.w	#$FFFF,BLTAFWM(a6)
	move.w	#$0000,BLTALWM(a6)
	move.l	a0,BLTAPT(a6)
	move.l	a1,BLTDPT(a6)
	move.w	#-2,BLTAMOD(a6)
	move.w	#36,BLTDMOD(a6)
	move.w	#(3<<6)+2,BLTSIZE(a6)
@hint
ASH = 4 gives BLTCON0 $49F0. The blit is 2 words wide: BLTALWM $0000, BLTAMOD -2, BLTDMOD 36.
@test Three rows shifted by 4
setup: a0=20000 m20000.w=FFFF m20002.w=8001 m20004.w=F00F a1=30000
expect: m30000.l=0FFFF000 m30028.l=08001000 m30050.l=0F00F000
@quiz
? Where does the A shift go?
+ The top 4 bits of BLTCON0
- BLTCON1 bits 0-3
- BLTAMOD
? Why is the destination one word wider than the image?
+ Bits shifted out of the last word need somewhere to go
- The blitter always adds a word
- For the mask

=== LESSON 83: Clearing with the blitter
@summary Using only channel D: clearing a whole bitplane while the CPU runs on.
@text
With only channel D enabled and the minterm $00, the blitter writes zeros: the fastest way to clear a screen, and the CPU is free while it happens.

	BLTCON0	$0100	USED, minterm 0
	BLTSIZE	256 << 6 | 20	256 rows of 20 words

BLTSIZE's height field is 10 bits: a value of 0 means 1024 rows, and the width field 0 means 64 words. A whole 320 x 256 plane fits in one blit. (ECS chips add BLTSIZV and BLTSIZH for bigger blits.)

Because the blitter runs by itself, the CPU must not change the blitter's registers - or use the memory it is writing - until BBUSY clears. Waiting before each blit, rather than after, lets the CPU do other work in between.
@task
Clear the whole 320 x 256 bitplane at A0 (40 bytes x 256 rows) with the blitter, waiting for it first.
@start
	lea	CUSTOM,a6
	; clear 40 x 256 bytes at A0 with channel D

@solution
	lea	CUSTOM,a6
	tst.b	DMACONR(a6)
waitblit:
	btst	#6,DMACONR(a6)
	bne	waitblit
	move.w	#$0100,BLTCON0(a6)
	move.w	#0,BLTCON1(a6)
	move.l	a0,BLTDPT(a6)
	move.w	#0,BLTDMOD(a6)
	move.w	#(256<<6)+20,BLTSIZE(a6)
@hint
BLTCON0 $0100, BLTDPT = A0, BLTDMOD 0, BLTSIZE (256 << 6) + 20.
@test A full bitplane
setup: a0=20000 m20000.l=FFFFFFFF m21400.l=FFFFFFFF m227FC.l=FFFFFFFF m22800.l=FFFFFFFF
expect: m20000.l=0 m21400.l=0 m227FC.l=0 m22800.l=FFFFFFFF uses=btst
@quiz
? What does a height of 0 in BLTSIZE mean?
- No blit
+ 1024 rows
- 256 rows
? Which BLTCON0 clears memory?
+ $0100
- $09F0
- $0FCA

=== LESSON 84: Filling shapes
@summary The blitter's area fill: outlines in, solid shapes out.
@text
The blitter can fill between edges as it writes. On each row it works from right to left; every set bit it meets switches the filling on or off. So a shape drawn as a pair of edge pixels on each row comes out solid - this is how Amiga vector graphics draw filled polygons so quickly.

	BLTCON1	$000A	IFE (inclusive fill, bit 3) + DESC (bit 1)
	BLTCON1	$0012	EFE (exclusive fill, bit 4) + DESC: the left edge pixel is dropped

Fill mode only works in descending mode, where the blitter runs backwards through memory: point A and D at the last word of the area. Filling can be done in place, with A and D the same.
@task
A0 points at an area 2 words wide (4 bytes per row, no gap between rows) and 3 rows high holding outlines. Fill it in place with an inclusive fill. Wait for the blitter first.
@start
	lea	CUSTOM,a6
	; inclusive fill of the 2 x 3 word area at A0, in place

@solution
	lea	CUSTOM,a6
	tst.b	DMACONR(a6)
waitblit:
	btst	#6,DMACONR(a6)
	bne	waitblit
	lea	10(a0),a1
	move.w	#$09F0,BLTCON0(a6)
	move.w	#$000A,BLTCON1(a6)
	move.w	#$FFFF,BLTAFWM(a6)
	move.w	#$FFFF,BLTALWM(a6)
	move.l	a1,BLTAPT(a6)
	move.l	a1,BLTDPT(a6)
	move.w	#0,BLTAMOD(a6)
	move.w	#0,BLTDMOD(a6)
	move.w	#(3<<6)+2,BLTSIZE(a6)
@hint
The last word is at A0 + 10. BLTCON0 $09F0 (A to D), BLTCON1 $000A, modulos 0, BLTSIZE (3 << 6) + 2.
@test Three rows of outlines
setup: a0=20000 m20000.l=08000010 m20004.l=0 m20008.l=80000001
expect: m20000.l=0FFFFFF0 m20004.l=0 m20008.l=FFFFFFFF
@quiz
? Which direction does the fill work along a row?
+ Right to left
- Left to right
- Top to bottom
? Why must a fill blit be descending?
+ The fill runs from the end of each row, and only descending mode goes that way
- To save memory
- Ascending mode has no masks

=== LESSON 85: Cookie-cut BOBs
@summary The minterm $CA: drawing a shape over the background through its mask.
@text
A BOB (blitter object) is drawn over a background without wiping out the pixels around it. Three sources do it:

	A	the mask: 1 where the BOB has a pixel
	B	the BOB's image
	C	the background (the screen)
	D	the screen again

D = (A AND B) OR (NOT A AND C): where the mask is set take the image, elsewhere keep the background. That function is minterm $CA, and with all four channels BLTCON0 is $0FCA. Because the mask is shifted along with the image (A and B use the same shift on the 68000's blitter, via ASH and BSH), it works at any x position.

This "cookie cut" is how the Amiga draws game characters in software: save the background, cut the BOB in, restore the background next frame.
@task
A0 points at a mask, A1 at an image (each 1 word wide, 2 rows, 2 bytes per row). A2 points at the screen (40 bytes wide). Cookie-cut the image into the screen at A2 with minterm $CA. Wait for the blitter first.
@start
	lea	CUSTOM,a6
	; D = (A AND B) OR (NOT A AND C) into the screen at A2

@solution
	lea	CUSTOM,a6
	tst.b	DMACONR(a6)
waitblit:
	btst	#6,DMACONR(a6)
	bne	waitblit
	move.w	#$0FCA,BLTCON0(a6)
	move.w	#0,BLTCON1(a6)
	move.w	#$FFFF,BLTAFWM(a6)
	move.w	#$FFFF,BLTALWM(a6)
	move.l	a0,BLTAPT(a6)
	move.l	a1,BLTBPT(a6)
	move.l	a2,BLTCPT(a6)
	move.l	a2,BLTDPT(a6)
	move.w	#0,BLTAMOD(a6)
	move.w	#0,BLTBMOD(a6)
	move.w	#38,BLTCMOD(a6)
	move.w	#38,BLTDMOD(a6)
	move.w	#(2<<6)+1,BLTSIZE(a6)
@hint
BLTCON0 $0FCA; A = A0, B = A1, C and D = A2. Modulos 0 for A and B, 38 for C and D. BLTSIZE (2 << 6) + 1.
@test Two rows
setup: a0=20000 m20000.w=FF00 m20002.w=FF0 a1=21000 m21000.w=AAFF m21002.w=FFFF a2=30000 m30000.w=1234 m30028.w=0
expect: m30000.w=AA34 m30028.w=FF0
@quiz
? Which minterm draws a BOB through its mask?
- $F0
+ $CA
- $0A

+ the background

=== LESSON 86: Hardware sprites
@summary The sprite control words: where a sprite is and how tall.
@text
The Amiga has 8 hardware sprites, 16 pixels wide and any height, drawn by the hardware on top of the playfield. Each sprite's data starts with two control words:

	POS	= VSTART bits 7-0 << 8  |  HSTART bits 8-1
	CTL	= VSTOP bits 7-0 << 8  |  VSTART bit 8 << 2  |  VSTOP bit 8 << 1  |  HSTART bit 0

then two words per line (the two bit planes of its 3 colours) and two zero words to end it.

For the standard screen, a sprite at screen position (x, y) has VSTART = y + $2C, HSTART = x + 128, and VSTOP = VSTART + height. The 9th bits matter: from line 212 of the screen VSTART is above 255, and the 9th bit goes in CTL.

To move a sprite you only rewrite its two control words - no drawing at all.
@task
Write the two control words for a sprite at x = D0, y = D1, D2 lines high (all words) to (A0) and 2(A0). D3 to D7 may be used as scratch.
@start
	; POS to (a0), CTL to 2(a0) for x = D0, y = D1, height D2

@solution
	move.w	d1,d3
	add.w	#$2C,d3
	move.w	d3,d4
	add.w	d2,d4
	move.w	d0,d5
	add.w	#128,d5
	move.w	d3,d6
	lsl.w	#8,d6
	move.w	d5,d7
	lsr.w	#1,d7
	move.b	d7,d6
	move.w	d6,(a0)
	move.w	d4,d6
	lsl.w	#8,d6
	btst	#8,d3
	beq	nov8
	bset	#2,d6
nov8:
	btst	#8,d4
	beq	nostop8
	bset	#1,d6
nostop8:
	btst	#0,d5
	beq	noh0
	bset	#0,d6
noh0:
	move.w	d6,2(a0)
@hint
VSTART in D3, VSTOP in D4, HSTART in D5. POS = (VSTART << 8) with HSTART >> 1 in the low byte. CTL = VSTOP << 8, then set bits 2, 1 and 0 from VSTART bit 8, VSTOP bit 8 and HSTART bit 0.
@test Top left, 16 high
setup: a0=3000 d0=0 d1=0 d2=10
expect: m3000.l=2C403C00
@test x = 161, y = 100, 8 high (odd HSTART)
setup: a0=3000 d0=A1 d1=64 d2=8
expect: m3000.l=90909801
@test Low on the screen: the 9th bits
setup: a0=3000 d0=32 d1=DC d2=14
expect: m3000.l=08591C06
@quiz
? How wide is an Amiga hardware sprite?
+ 16 pixels
- 32 pixels
- 8 pixels
? How many colours does a single sprite have, plus transparent?
+ 3
- 15
- 1

=== LESSON 87: Sprite pointers and DMA
@summary SPRxPT, a null sprite for the unused channels, and SPREN.
@text
Each sprite channel has a pointer, SPR0PT to SPR7PT, at $120 to $13C. Like the bitplane pointers they are used up as the sprite is drawn, so they must be rewritten every frame - normally by the copper.

Once sprite DMA is on (SPREN, bit 5 of DMACON), all eight channels fetch data. A channel nobody set up would show rubbish, so point every unused channel at a null sprite: two zero words, which end it at once.

	null:	dc.l	0
@task
Point sprite 0 at the sprite data at A0, point sprites 1 to 7 at the null sprite at A1, and switch on sprite DMA together with the master switch.
@start
	; SPR0PT = A0, SPR1PT-SPR7PT = A1, DMA on

@solution
	lea	CUSTOM+SPR0PT,a2
	move.l	a0,(a2)+
	moveq	#6,d0
loop:
	move.l	a1,(a2)+
	dbra	d0,loop
	move.w	#$8220,CUSTOM+DMACON
@hint
The pointers are consecutive longs from CUSTOM+SPR0PT. DMACON $8220 sets DMAEN and SPREN.
@test Eight pointers and DMA
setup: a0=3000 a1=3100
expect: hwlong120=3000 hwlong124=3100 hwlong13C=3100 hwcount140=0 dmacon=220
@quiz
? Why point unused sprite channels at a null sprite?
+ Otherwise they fetch whatever their pointer happens to point at
- To save power
- The copper needs it
? Which DMACON bit is sprite DMA?
+ Bit 5 (SPREN)
- Bit 8 (BPLEN)
- Bit 6 (BLTEN)

=== LESSON 88: Paula: playing a sound
@summary Audio DMA: a sample's address, length, period and volume.
@text
Paula plays four channels of 8-bit samples by DMA. Each channel has:

	AUDxLC	the sample's address (chip RAM, even)
	AUDxLEN	its length in words
	AUDxPER	the period: clock cycles per sample (PAL: 3546895 / rate)
	AUDxVOL	volume 0 to 64

then DMACON bit 0 to 3 (AUD0EN to AUD3EN) with DMAEN starts it. A period of 428 plays at about 8287 samples a second - the note C-3 in a tracker. The sample loops: when it ends, Paula reloads AUDxLC and AUDxLEN and goes round again, so to play a sound once you set a short silent loop after it starts (ptplayer does this for you in ADM's PLAY SFX).

Channels 0 and 3 are the left speaker, 1 and 2 the right.
@task
Play the sample at A0, D0 bytes long (even), on channel 0: period 428, full volume, then switch on its DMA.
@start
	lea	CUSTOM,a6
	; AUD0LC, AUD0LEN (words), AUD0PER, AUD0VOL, then DMA

@solution
	lea	CUSTOM,a6
	move.l	a0,AUD0LC(a6)
	lsr.w	#1,d0
	move.w	d0,AUD0LEN(a6)
	move.w	#428,AUD0PER(a6)
	move.w	#64,AUD0VOL(a6)
	move.w	#$8201,DMACON(a6)
@hint
The length register counts words: halve the byte count. DMACON $8201 sets DMAEN and AUD0EN.
@test An 8 KB sample
setup: a0=30000 d0=2000
expect: hwlong0A0=30000 hw0A4=1000 hw0A6=1AC hw0A8=40 dmacon=201
@test A short sample
setup: a0=12346 d0=100
expect: hwlong0A0=12346 hw0A4=80 dmacon=201
@quiz
? What does AUDxLEN count?
- Bytes
+ Words
- Samples per second

+ 0 and 3
- 0 and 1
- 2 and 3

=== LESSON 89: Reading the joystick
@summary JOY1DAT's odd encoding and the fire button on CIA-A.
@text
A joystick in port 2 is read from JOY1DAT. Left and right are bits 9 and 1; up and down are coded with an exclusive or:

	right	= bit 1
	left	= bit 9
	down	= bit 1 EOR bit 0
	up	= bit 9 EOR bit 8

Shift the value right by one and EOR it with itself: bit 0 of the result is then "down" and bit 8 is "up". The fire button is bit 7 of CIA-A's port A ($BFE001) - and it reads 0 when pressed.

	btst	#7,CIAA+CIAPRA	; Z set = fire pressed
@task
Read the joystick in port 2. Set D0 to the x direction (-1 left, 1 right, 0 neither), D1 to the y direction (-1 up, 1 down, 0 neither) and D2 to 1 if fire is pressed, else 0. D3 and D4 may be used as scratch.
@start
	; D0 = x direction, D1 = y direction, D2 = fire

@solution
	move.w	CUSTOM+JOY1DAT,d3
	moveq	#0,d0
	moveq	#0,d1
	btst	#1,d3
	beq	notright
	moveq	#1,d0
notright:
	btst	#9,d3
	beq	notleft
	moveq	#-1,d0
notleft:
	move.w	d3,d4
	lsr.w	#1,d4
	eor.w	d3,d4
	btst	#0,d4
	beq	notdown
	moveq	#1,d1
notdown:
	btst	#8,d4
	beq	notup
	moveq	#-1,d1
notup:
	moveq	#0,d2
	btst	#7,CIAA+CIAPRA
	bne	nofire
	moveq	#1,d2
nofire:
@hint
Right and left are plain bits. For up and down, copy JOY1DAT, shift the copy right by 1 and EOR the original into it: bit 0 = down, bit 8 = up. Fire: btst #7,CIAA+CIAPRA - pressed when Z is set.
@test Right and fire
setup: joy=right+fire d0=55 d1=55 d2=55
expect: d0=1 d1=0 d2=1
@test Left and up
setup: joy=left+up
expect: d0=FFFFFFFF d1=FFFFFFFF d2=0
@test Down
setup: joy=down
expect: d0=0 d1=1 d2=0
@test Right and down
setup: joy=right+down
expect: d0=1 d1=1 d2=0
@test Nothing pressed
setup: d0=55 d1=55 d2=55
expect: d0=0 d1=0 d2=0
@quiz
? What does bit 7 of $BFE001 read when the fire button is pressed?
- 1
+ 0
? Which bits of JOY1DAT give "up"?
+ Bit 9 EOR bit 8
- Bit 8 only
- Bit 0

=== LESSON 90: CIA timers
@summary Programming CIA-A's timer A: a countdown in E-clock ticks.
@text
Each CIA has two 16-bit timers that count down at the E clock, 709,379 Hz on a PAL Amiga (one tenth of the CPU clock). A timer is loaded from a latch written in two halves and controlled by a control register:

	TALO	$BFE401	timer A low byte
	TAHI	$BFE501	timer A high byte
	CRA	$BFEE01	control: bit 0 START, bit 3 one-shot, bit 4 force LOAD

For a tick 50 times a second, load 709379 / 50 = 14187 ($376B). When it reaches zero it can raise an interrupt (through the CIA's ICR, Paula level 2 for CIA-A) and reloads itself unless it is in one-shot mode. ptplayer uses CIA-B's timers to play music at the right tempo whatever the screen rate.

The CIA registers are bytes, one every $100: CIAA+CIATALO, CIAA+CIATAHI, CIAA+CIACRA.
@task
Load CIA-A timer A with 14187 ($376B): the low byte, then the high byte. Then start it continuous with a forced load: write $11 to CRA.
@start
	; TALO = $6B, TAHI = $37, CRA = $11

@solution
	move.b	#$6B,CIAA+CIATALO
	move.b	#$37,CIAA+CIATAHI
	move.b	#$11,CIAA+CIACRA
@hint
Three byte writes: CIAA+CIATALO, CIAA+CIATAHI, CIAA+CIACRA.
@test Timer A for 50 Hz
setup: d0=0
expect: ciaa4=6B ciaa5=37 ciaa14=11
@quiz
? How fast does a PAL CIA timer count?
+ 709,379 Hz (the E clock)
- 7.09 MHz
- 50 Hz
? Which bit of CRA starts the timer?
+ Bit 0
- Bit 4
- Bit 7

=== LESSON 91: The 68020: EXTB.L
@summary What the A1200's 68020 adds, starting with a one-step byte to long.
@text
The A1200 has a 68020: 32-bit throughout, a small instruction cache, faster at almost everything, and with new instructions and addressing modes. Code for the 68000 runs unchanged; the new instructions only work on a 68020 or later. This lesson and the next five are assembled and run as 68020 code.

EXTB.L sign-extends a byte straight to a long, where the 68000 needs EXT.W then EXT.L:

	extb.l	d0		; $123456F0 -> $FFFFFFF0

@task

@start
	; D0 and D1: signed byte -> long

@solution
	extb.l	d0
	extb.l	d1
@hint
extb.l d0 and extb.l d1.
@test Negative and positive

@test Extremes

@quiz

=== LESSON 92: Scaled indexes
@summary (d8,An,Dn*4): the 68020 multiplies the index for you.
@text
On the 68000, a table of words or longs needs the index doubled or quadrupled first (lesson 32). The 68020 scales it in the addressing mode:

	move.l	0(a0,d0.w*4),d1		; long entry D0
	move.w	0(a0,d0.w*2),d1		; word entry D0

@start

@hint

=== LESSON 93: 32-bit multiply and divide
@summary MULU.L and DIVUL.L: no more 16-bit limits.
@text
The 68000's MULU and DIVU work on words. The 68020 adds long forms:

	mulu.l	d1,d0		; D0 = D0 * D1 (low 32 bits; V if it overflowed)
	mulu.l	d1,d2:d0	; D2:D0 = the full 64-bit product
	divu.l	d1,d0		; D0 = D0 / D1 (quotient only)
	divul.l	d1,d4:d0	; D0 = D0 / D1, D4 = remainder
	divu.l	d1,d2:d0	; 64-bit dividend D2:D0

The signed versions are MULS.L, DIVS.L and DIVSL.L. They are also faster than doing it by hand: a 32 x 32 multiply on a 68000 takes several MULUs and adds.
@cpu 68020
@task
Set D2 to D0 x D1 (the low 32 bits, unsigned), and D3 to D0 / D1 with the remainder in D4 (unsigned longs). D0 and D1 must be unchanged.
@start
	; D2 = D0 * D1, D3 = D0 / D1, D4 = remainder

@solution
	move.l	d0,d2
	mulu.l	d1,d2
	move.l	d0,d3
	divul.l	d1,d4:d3
@hint
Copy D0 to D2 and mulu.l d1,d2. Copy D0 to D3 and divul.l d1,d4:d3.

@quiz

+ D4
- D0
- D1

=== LESSON 94: Bit fields
@summary BFEXTU and BFINS: reading and writing fields of any width at any position.
@text
The 68020's bit field instructions work on a run of 1 to 32 bits at any offset, in a register or in memory:

	bfextu	d0{22:5},d1	; D1 = 5 bits of D0 starting at offset 22
	bfins	d2,d0{27:5}	; put the low 5 bits of D2 there

The offset counts from the most significant bit: in a register, offset 0 is bit 31, so bit n is at offset 31 - n. A field at bits 9 to 5 starts at offset 31 - 9 = 22. In memory, offset 0 is the top bit of the byte at the address, and fields can cross bytes.

Packed graphics data - colour components, tile numbers, sprite flags - becomes much easier. A 15-bit colour has red in bits 14-10, green in 9-5 and blue in 4-0.
@cpu 68020
@task
D0 holds a 15-bit colour (red bits 14-10, green 9-5, blue 4-0). Put the green value into D1 with BFEXTU, then replace the blue value with the low 5 bits of D2 using BFINS.
@start
	; D1 = green of D0, then blue of D0 = D2

@solution
	bfextu	d0{22:5},d1
	bfins	d2,d0{27:5}
@hint
Green is bits 9-5: offset 22, width 5. Blue is bits 4-0: offset 27, width 5.
@test $1234
setup: d0=1234 d2=3 d1=0
expect: d1=11 d0=1223 uses=bfextu+bfins
@test Full green
setup: d0=3E0 d2=1F d1=55
expect: d1=1F d0=3FF
@quiz
? In a data register, which bit is at bit field offset 0?
+ Bit 31
- Bit 0
- Bit 7

=== LESSON 95: Finding the first set bit: BFFFO
@summary BFFFO: the position of the highest 1 bit, in one instruction.
@text
BFFFO ("find first one") returns the offset of the first set bit in a field, scanning from the most significant end. If the field is all zeros, it returns the offset plus the width, and sets Z.

	bfffo	d0{0:32},d1	; D1 = number of leading zeros of D0

For a whole register the number of the highest set bit is then 31 - D1. That answers "which is the most important flag set?", gives a quick log2 for scaling numbers and finds free entries in allocation bitmaps - all in one instruction where the 68000 needs a loop.
@cpu 68020
@task
Set D1 to the number (0 to 31) of the highest set bit in D0, or to -1 if D0 is zero.
@start
	; D1 = number of the highest set bit of D0, or -1

@solution
	bfffo	d0{0:32},d1
	beq	zero
	neg.l	d1
	add.l	#31,d1
	bra	done
zero:
	moveq	#-1,d1
done:
@hint
After bfffo d0{0:32},d1, Z is set if D0 was zero. Otherwise the bit number is 31 - D1.
@test Bit 0
setup: d0=1
expect: d1=0 uses=bfffo
@test Bit 31
setup: d0=80000000
expect: d1=1F
@test $12345
setup: d0=12345
expect: d1=10
@test Zero
setup: d0=0 d1=5
expect: d1=FFFFFFFF
@quiz
? What does BFFFO return for an all-zero field?
+ The offset plus the width, with Z set
- -1
- 0

=== LESSON 96: AGA colours
@summary 24-bit colours on the A1200: high and low nibbles through BPLCON3.
@text
AGA, the A1200's chipset, has 256 colour registers of 24 bits each - but the registers are still the old 12-bit COLORxx words. BPLCON3 chooses what a write means:

	bits 15-13	BANK: which 32 of the 256 colours COLOR00-COLOR31 reach
	bit 9		LOCT: 0 = write the high nibbles, 1 = the low nibbles

For the colour $RrGgBb, write $0RGB (the high nibbles) with LOCT = 0, then $0rgb (the low nibbles) with LOCT = 1. Writing the high nibbles also copies them into the low ones, so on an older chipset - or if you skip the second write - you still get the nearest 12-bit colour. Always write high first.
@task

@start

	; COLOR00 = $123456 on AGA

@solution
	lea	CUSTOM,a6
	move.w	#$0000,BPLCON3(a6)
	move.w	#$0135,COLOR00(a6)
	move.w	#$0200,BPLCON3(a6)
	move.w	#$0246,COLOR00(a6)
@hint
$123456 has high nibbles 1, 3, 5 ($0135) and low nibbles 2, 4, 6 ($0246). LOCT is bit 9: $0200.

=== LESSON 97: AGA bitplanes and FMODE
@summary Eight bitplanes and the faster fetch modes.
@text
AGA displays up to 8 bitplanes - 256 colours. BPLCON0's plane count is 3 bits (BPU2-BPU0, bits 14-12) plus a fourth, BPU3, in bit 4; 8 planes is BPU = 0 with BPU3 set:

	1-7 planes	BPLCON0 = planes << 12 | $0200
	8 planes	BPLCON0 = $0210

FMODE ($DFF1FC) sets how many bits Agnus fetches at once: 0 is the old 16, 1 and 2 are 32, 3 is 64. Wider fetches leave more chip bus time for the CPU and blitter, and are needed for 8 planes at high resolutions - but bitplane data and widths must then be aligned to 64 bits. ECS registers ignore all of this, which is why an AGA screen shows rubbish on an A500.
@task
Write BPLCON0 for D0 bitplanes (1 to 8), the AGA way, then set FMODE to 3.
@start
	; BPLCON0 for D0 planes (8 = $0210), FMODE = 3

@solution
	cmp.w	#8,d0
	bne	notall
	move.w	#$0210,CUSTOM+BPLCON0
	bra	fetch
notall:
	move.w	d0,d1
	moveq	#12,d2
	lsl.w	d2,d1
	or.w	#$0200,d1
	move.w	d1,CUSTOM+BPLCON0
fetch:
	move.w	#3,CUSTOM+FMODE
@hint
Special-case 8 ($0210); otherwise the old formula. Then move.w #3,CUSTOM+FMODE.
@test 8 planes
setup: d0=8
expect: hw100=210 hw1FC=3
@test 6 planes
setup: d0=6
expect: hw100=6200 hw1FC=3
@quiz
? Which BPLCON0 value gives 8 bitplanes with colour?
+ $0210
- $8200
- $0800
? What does FMODE 3 do?
+ Fetches 64 bits at a time
- Enables 3 planes
- Turns sprites off

=== LESSON 98: Making it fast
@summary A whole-loop optimisation: replacing MULU with shifts and adds.
@text
Speed on the 68000 comes from a few habits: keep values in registers, avoid MULU and DIVU in inner loops, use (An)+ to step through data, unroll, and pick the cheap form of each instruction (MOVEQ, ADDQ, LEA). Measure every change - the simulator's cycle count is there for that.

Multiplying by a constant is the commonest win. x * 10 = x * 8 + x * 2:

	add.w	d0,d0		; x2
	move.w	d0,d1
	add.w	d0,d0		; x4
	add.w	d0,d0		; x8
	add.w	d1,d0		; x8 + x2

ADD.W Dn,Dn takes 4 cycles; MULU.W #10 takes about 46.
@task
Multiply each of the 32 words at A0 by 10, in place (the results fit in a word). The code in the editor works but takes about 2300 cycles; the limit is 1800. D0, D1 and D2 may be used.
@start
	moveq	#31,d2
loop:
	move.w	(a0),d0
	mulu.w	#10,d0
	move.w	d0,(a0)+
	dbra	d2,loop
@solution
	moveq	#31,d2
loop:
	move.w	(a0),d0
	add.w	d0,d0
	move.w	d0,d1
	add.w	d0,d0
	add.w	d0,d0
	add.w	d1,d0
	move.w	d0,(a0)+
	dbra	d2,loop
@hint
Replace the MULU with the shift-and-add sequence from the text.
@test 32 words, fast
setup: a0=2000 m2000.w=1 m2002.w=7 m203C.w=64 m203E.w=FF m2040.w=1234
expect: m2000.w=A m2002.w=46 m203C.w=3E8 m203E.w=9F6 m2040.w=1234 maxcyc=1800
@quiz
? Why avoid MULU in an inner loop on a 68000?
+ It takes 38 to 70 cycles, where shifts and adds take a few
- It gives wrong answers
- It only works on words

=== LESSON 99: Final project, part 1: moving the player
@summary Joystick to movement: a player that moves 2 pixels a frame and stays on the screen.
@text
Time to put the pieces together. A game frame reads the input, moves things, and draws. This part is the movement.

The player is a record at A0: x (word) then y (word), in screen pixels. Each frame, read the joystick as in lesson 89 and move 2 pixels in that direction. Keep the player on the screen: x from 0 to 304 and y from 0 to 240 (a 16 x 16 sprite on a 320 x 256 screen).

Clamping after adding is simplest: if the new x is negative, make it 0; if above 304, make it 304. A signed compare is needed - x can go below zero for a moment.
@task
Move the player at A0 (x word, y word) by 2 pixels in the joystick's direction, then clamp x to 0..304 and y to 0..240. D0 to D4 may be used.
@start
	; read JOY1DAT, move x,y at (a0) by 2 per direction, clamp

@solution
	move.w	CUSTOM+JOY1DAT,d3
	moveq	#0,d0
	moveq	#0,d1
	btst	#1,d3
	beq	nr
	moveq	#2,d0
nr:
	btst	#9,d3
	beq	nl
	moveq	#-2,d0
nl:
	move.w	d3,d4
	lsr.w	#1,d4
	eor.w	d3,d4
	btst	#0,d4
	beq	nd
	moveq	#2,d1
nd:
	btst	#8,d4
	beq	nu
	moveq	#-2,d1
nu:
	move.w	(a0),d2
	add.w	d0,d2
	bpl	xpos
	moveq	#0,d2
xpos:
	cmp.w	#304,d2
	ble	xok
	move.w	#304,d2
xok:
	move.w	d2,(a0)
	move.w	2(a0),d2
	add.w	d1,d2
	bpl	ypos
	moveq	#0,d2
ypos:
	cmp.w	#240,d2
	ble	yok
	move.w	#240,d2
yok:
	move.w	d2,2(a0)
@hint
